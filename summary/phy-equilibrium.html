Equilibrium & Moments

Physics for Engineering · M.4 · Chapter 6 / 7

When does a body stay perfectly still? Force balance, Lami's theorem for three forces, the moment of a force and the principle of moments.

Conditions for equilibrium

A body is in static equilibrium when it neither accelerates nor rotates. Two independent conditions must hold together:

1 · TranslationalΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0. No net force in any direction, so no linear acceleration.
2 · RotationalΣM = 0 about any point. No net turning effect, so no angular acceleration.

Kinds of equilibrium

TypeWhen disturbed…Example
Stablereturns to its original positioncone resting on its base
Unstablemoves further awaycone balanced on its tip
Neutralstays in the new positionball on a flat table

Lami's theorem

For three concurrent coplanar forces in equilibrium, each force divided by the sine of the angle between the other two is the same number:

F₁ / sin α₁ = F₂ / sin α₂ = F₃ / sin α₃α₁ is opposite F₁ (between F₂ and F₃); α₁ + α₂ + α₃ = 360°

It follows from the sine rule on the closed force triangle that three balanced forces form. It works for exactly three forces, and cannot be extended directly to four or more. Change the angles in Fig. 1 and watch the force triangle close.

Moment of a force

The moment (torque) of a force about a pivot is its turning effect:

M = F × dd = perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of F; unit N·m

By convention anticlockwise moments are positive and clockwise negative. If the force is not perpendicular to the arm, use its perpendicular component: M = F sin θ · l. A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero moment.

FormWhenExample
M = Fdforce perpendicular to the armspanner on a bolt
M = F sin θ · lforce at angle θ to an arm of length ldoor pushed at an angle
M = 0force through the pivotpushing straight at a hinge

Principle of moments

For rotational equilibrium, the sum of the moments that turn one way about any pivot equals the sum that turn the other way about the same pivot: Σ(clockwise) = Σ(anticlockwise). It holds for any pivot you choose, which is a powerful trick: put the pivot where an unknown force acts so its moment disappears.

Worked example: F₁ = 40 N at d₁ = 0.9 m on one side, F₂ at d₂ = 0.6 m on the other. Balance means 40 × 0.9 = F₂ × 0.6, so F₂ = 60 N. Set the sliders in Fig. 2 to these values and the plank balances.

Worked example: the hanging sign

A 200 N sign hangs from the midpoint of a horizontal bar 1.5 m long. The bar is pinned to a wall at one end and held by a cable at 40° to the bar at the other end. Find the cable tension T and the wall reaction R.

StepWorking
1 Moments about the wall pinChoose the pin as the pivot so the unknown wall force drops out: T sin 40° × 1.5 − 200 × 0.75 = 0, so T ≈ 155.6 N.
2 Horizontal balanceThe cable pulls the bar toward the wall with T cos 40° ≈ 119.2 N, so the wall pushes out with Rx ≈ 119.2 N.
3 Vertical balanceRy + T sin 40° = 200, so Ry ≈ 200 − 100 = 100 N upward.

The defaults in Fig. 3 reproduce these numbers. In general T = W·(fraction of L)/sin φ, so a flatter cable (small φ) needs a much larger tension.

import math
def check_equilibrium(forces):            # forces: list of (Fx, Fy)
    return sum(f[0] for f in forces), sum(f[1] for f in forces)
T = 155.6
forces = [(T*math.cos(math.radians(40)), T*math.sin(math.radians(40))),
          (0, -200), (-119.2, -100)]
print(check_equilibrium(forces))          # ≈ (0.0, 0.0)
