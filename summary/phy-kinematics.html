Kinematics & SUVAT

Physics for Engineering · M.4 · Chapter 1 / 7

Describing motion with position, velocity and acceleration, no forces needed: scalars versus vectors, the four SUVAT equations, motion graphs and free fall.

What is kinematics?

Kinematics describes how things move, not why. The why belongs to dynamics and Newton's laws. We treat motion purely as position, velocity and acceleration changing with time: no forces and no masses, just the geometry of motion.

QuantityMeaningType
Position xlocation on a number line (needs a reference point)vector
Displacement Δxchange in position, xf − xivector
Distancetotal path length travelled, always positivescalar
Speedmagnitude of velocity: how fast, not which wayscalar
Velocity vrate of change of displacement; sign gives directionvector
Acceleration arate of change of velocity; positive = speeding up (in the + direction)vector
Scalarmagnitude only. One number and a unit is enough: distance 15 m, speed 30 m/s.
Vectormagnitude and direction: displacement 15 m to the right, velocity 30 m/s north. In one dimension direction is just a + or − sign.
Common slipRun once round a 400 m track and back to the start: distance = 400 m but displacement = 0, so the average velocity is also 0 while the average speed is not.

The slope of an x–t graph is velocity. The slope of a v–t graph is acceleration, and the area under a v–t graph is displacement. These graph facts are the most useful tools in the chapter; see them in action in Fig. 1.

The SUVAT equations

When acceleration is constant, five variables are linked: s displacement, u initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration and t time. Know any three and you can find the other two. Each equation leaves one variable out, which tells you which to pick.

EquationMissing variableUse when
v = u + atsdisplacement is unknown and not needed
s = ut + ½at²vfinal velocity is unknown and not needed
v² = u² + 2asttime is unknown and not needed
s = ½(u + v)taacceleration is unknown and not needed

Method. 1) Write the five letters. 2) Fill in what is given (decide + direction first, so a falling object has a = +10 if down is positive). 3) Circle what you want. 4) Pick the equation that omits the letter you neither have nor want.

Check the numbers: the panel shows v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², and two cross-checks (s = ½(u + v)t and v² = u² + 2as) that always agree.

Free fall

Near the Earth's surface every object accelerates downward at g = 10 m/s² in class (9.8 m/s² more precisely), with air resistance ignored. Take downward as positive and a = +10. A dropped object has u = 0; a thrown object has u ≠ 0, and a stone thrown up at 20 m/s has a = −10 if up is positive.

Worked example: dropped from rest

Given u = 0, a = 10 m/s², t = 3 s. Velocity: v = 0 + 10 × 3 = 30 m/s. Displacement: s = ½ × 10 × 3² = 45 m.

Thrown upward

At the top v = 0, so tpeak = u/g and H = u²/(2g). The way up and down are symmetric: it returns to its starting height with the same speed u, after 2u/g. Set u = 20, h₀ = 0 in Fig. 2: time to top 2 s, maximum height 20 m, landing after 4 s.

u, a, t_end = 0, 10, 3
for t in range(t_end + 1):
    v = u + a*t
    s = u*t + 0.5*a*t**2
    print(f"t={t}s: v={v} m/s, s={s} m")
# t=0s: v=0 m/s, s=0.0 m ... t=3s: v=30 m/s, s=45.0 m
