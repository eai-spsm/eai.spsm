Work & Energy

Physics for Engineering · M.4 · Chapter 5 / 7

How forces transfer energy: work, kinetic and potential energy, conservation of mechanical energy and power as the rate of doing work.

On this page
  1. Work
  2. Kinetic energy
  3. Potential energy
  4. Conservation of mechanical energy
  5. Power

Work

Work is done when a force produces displacement along the force. W = F s cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. The unit is the joule (J = N·m) and work is a scalar.

Caseθcos θWork
Force along the motion0°1W = Fs, the maximum
Force at an angleθcos θW = Fs cos θ
Force across the motion90°0W = 0 (carrying a bag horizontally)
Force against the motion180°−1W = −Fs (friction, braking)

Negative work removes energy from an object. Friction does negative work, so it always takes kinetic energy away.

Kinetic energy

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion: KE = ½mv², always positive or zero. It comes straight from Newton's second law and kinematics.

The work–energy theorem

Start with W = Fs = mas and v² = u² + 2as, so as = (v² − u²)/2. Then W = m(v² − u²)/2 = ½mv² − ½mu². In words: Wnet = ΔKE, the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.

ObjectMassSpeedKE
Bicycle15 kg6 m/s½ × 15 × 36 = 270 J
Car1000 kg20 m/s½ × 1000 × 400 = 200 000 J
Bullet0.01 kg400 m/s½ × 0.01 × 160 000 = 800 J

Because KE depends on v², doubling the speed quadruples the energy. That is why stopping distance grows so fast at high speed.

Potential energy

Potential energy is stored energy from position or shape. Gravitational PE = mgh, measured from a chosen reference level. Elastic PE of a spring = ½kx², with k the spring constant and x the stretch or compression.

TypeFormulaDepends on
Gravitationalmghmass and height above the reference level
Elastic (spring)½kx²stiffness k and extension x
Chemical—energy stored in molecular bonds

PE and KE convert into each other. Rising raises PE and lowers KE; falling does the opposite. Without friction the total stays constant. The reference level for h is your choice, and only changes in PE matter.

Conservation of mechanical energy

If only conservative forces act (no friction or air drag) the total mechanical energy stays constant:

E = ½mv² + mgh = constantbetween any two points: ½mv₁² + mgh₁ = ½mv₂² + mgh₂

Friction is non-conservative: it turns mechanical energy into heat. Then Efinal = Einitial − Wfriction, with Wfriction = f·d for sliding distance d. The track in Fig. 2 shows all of it: the bars give PE, KE, heat and the constant total.

def energy(m, v, h, g=10):
    KE, PE = 0.5*m*v**2, m*g*h
    return KE, PE, KE + PE
print(energy(2, 3, 5))          # (9.0, 100, 109.0)
v_ground = (2*109/2) ** 0.5
print(energy(2, v_ground, 0))   # (109.0, 0, 109.0)

Power

Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy: P = W/t = ΔE/t. For a constant force along the velocity, P = Fv. The unit is the watt (W = J/s) and power is a scalar.
