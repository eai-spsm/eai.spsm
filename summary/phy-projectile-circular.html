Projectile & Circular Motion

Physics for Engineering · M.4 · Chapter 4 / 7

Two-dimensional motion: split a projectile into independent horizontal and vertical parts, then study uniform circular motion and the centripetal force.

Projectile motion basics

A projectile is anything launched into the air and moving under gravity alone: no engine and no thrust. The idea that makes it solvable is that horizontal and vertical motion are completely independent. Treat each direction separately and combine at the end through the shared time t.

DirectionForceAccelerationVelocity
Horizontalnone (ignoring air)0constant: vₓ = v₀cos θ
Verticalgravity, downwardg = 10 m/s² downchanging: vy = v₀sin θ − gt

The path is a parabola. Open Fig. 1, change the launch speed and angle, and press play. The two arrows on the ball are vₓ (never changes) and vy (shrinks, passes zero at the top, then grows downward).

Equations of projectile motion

QuantityHorizontal (x)Vertical (y)
Velocityvₓ = v₀cos θ (constant)vy = v₀sin θ − gt
Positionx = v₀cos θ · ty = v₀sin θ · t − ½gt²
Speed at time tv = √(vₓ² + vy²)

At the top the vertical velocity is zero: vy = 0, so tpeak = v₀sin θ / g. For a symmetric launch (landing at the launch height) the total time is twice that: T = 2v₀sin θ / g. Check the panel in Fig. 1: with v₀ = 20 and θ = 30° the time of flight is 2 s.

Range and maximum height

Substitute the peak time to get H directly, and use T to get the range R, so you need not solve for time each time.

H = v₀² sin²θ / (2g)maximum height, from y at t = v₀sinθ / g
R = v₀cosθ · (2v₀sinθ / g) = v₀² sin 2θ / guses 2 sinθ cosθ = sin 2θ

Complementary angles. θ and 90° − θ give the same range because sin 2θ = sin(180° − 2θ). In the diagram switch "Compare with 90° − θ" on: with h₀ = 0 the two paths land together; with h₀ > 0 they no longer do.

Worked example: projectile

A ball is launched at 20 m/s at 30° from level ground. Find the time of flight, the maximum height and the range.

StepWorking
1 Resolve the velocityvₓ = 20 cos 30° ≈ 17.3 m/s; vy0 = 20 sin 30° = 10 m/s
2 Time of flightT = 2vy0/g = 2 × 10/10 = 2 s
3 Maximum heightH = vy0²/(2g) = 100/20 = 5 m
4 RangeR = vₓT = 17.3 × 2 ≈ 34.6 m (check: 400 × sin 60°/10 = 34.6 ✓)
import math
def projectile(v0, theta_deg, g=10):
    th = math.radians(theta_deg)
    vx, vy0 = v0*math.cos(th), v0*math.sin(th)
    T = 2*vy0/g
    return T, vy0**2/(2*g), vx*T
print(projectile(20, 30))   # (2.0, 5.0, 34.64)

Uniform circular motion

An object moving round a circle at constant speed is still accelerating, because velocity is a vector and its direction keeps changing. That acceleration, the centripetal acceleration (centre-seeking), always points to the centre. By Newton's second law there must be a net force in that direction too: the centripetal force. It is not a new kind of force but whatever real force (tension, friction, gravity, normal force) points inward.
