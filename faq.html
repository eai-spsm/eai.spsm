Applying

10 questions

Is prior programming experience required?

No. There is no practical examination, and instructors introduce the fundamentals from the beginning of the program.

What GPA do I need?

A cumulative GPA of 2.00 or above over 5 semesters. The same applies to internal and external applicants.

How are applicants scored?

Mathematics 30%, Science 25%, English 25%, Interview 10% and Thai 10%.

How many students are admitted per cohort?

About 25 students: around 10 seats for internal (Prasarnmit) students and 15 for external applicants.

What are the admission rounds?

Round 1 is the main round, open to internal students for all five majors with one as the first choice. Round 2 is a supplementary round held when seats remain, and it is not offered every year. Round 3 is the external round, where internal students who missed the first two rounds can apply alongside external applicants. Details on How to apply.

How many chances do internal students get?

Three opportunities to enter before competing with external applicants, under the same requirements.

Can students from other schools apply?

Yes. About 15 seats are for external applicants, under the same requirements and criteria.

Is a portfolio required, and what should it include?

Not mandatory, though recommended. Include your academic record, extracurricular activities, projects and competition results. Computer-related work receives additional consideration during review.

Can I visit before applying?

Yes. The Open House is on 24 January 2027, where the major shows its work. Follow the LINE Openchat for updates.

I still have a question. Who do I ask?

Ask in the LINE Openchat or message us on Instagram.

The major

7 questions

What is the AI Engineering major?

A Mathayom 4 to 6 major at Satit Prasarnmit (Secondary) that builds knowledge and technical ability across computing, automation, robotics and AI, combining theory with hands-on practice in preparation for higher education. See About.

How does this differ from the Computer Science major?

The curriculum emphasizes applied, hands-on learning, from foundational engineering and programming to real-world problem solving with robotics and microcontrollers, including coursework certified by FIBO, KMUTT.

What will I learn each year?

Year 1 (M.4) covers foundations: code, circuits and first robots. Year 2 (M.5) is applied AI: vision, deep learning and single-board computers. Year 3 (M.6) is the capstone: independent research, robotics and the major project. Lesson write-ups are on Major Class.

How heavy is the timetable?

Every term has 40 periods a week, and credits run from 14.0 to 15.5 a semester. Core subjects such as Thai, mathematics, science, social studies and English continue alongside the major subjects.

Does the major include robotics and hardware?

Yes. Students study electrical circuits and microcontrollers, Physics in Robotics Technology and Microcontrollers & AI, and build robots in class. Some coursework is certified by FIBO, KMUTT.

What tools do we use?

Python and other languages, code editors, AI assistants, ML libraries and cloud platforms. The full list is on the Tools page.

Is there a big project?

Yes. M.6 has the AI Engineering Major Project, and every student develops a project of personal interest and presents it at an exhibition.

Graduating and activities

4 questions

What do I need to finish the major?

By the first semester of M.6: attend the major camp at least once and help run the open-house presentation, attend at least 2 skill-development activities, enter or compete in a technology contest, join a school-arranged site visit, present a personal project at an exhibition, and submit one English proficiency test result.

Do I need an English test score?

You need to submit at least one English proficiency test result (such as IELTS, TOEIC, CU-TEP or TU-GET) by M.6 semester 1. No minimum score is required.

Do I have to enter competitions?

Entering an innovation or invention contest in engineering, computing or technology is one of the graduation requirements. Past results are on the Works page.

What activities are offered?

Study visits and workshops, 3 to 5 field trips a year, a yearly 3-day 2-night major camp, and robotics and project competitions. See Events.

After graduating

4 questions

Where do graduates go next?

Over 72% of alumni are admitted through TCAS Round 1, most continuing into engineering faculties.

Which universities do alumni attend?

Of 122 alumni, 119 have a placement. The most common are KMITL (34), Chulalongkorn (32), KMUTT (19), Thammasat (14) and Kasetsart (6). See Universities Stats.

What careers can this lead to?

AI / ML engineer, computer vision engineer, robotics engineer, embedded engineer or data scientist, among others. The About page maps each career to its subjects.

Can I study something other than engineering?

Yes. Most alumni choose engineering, but some went on to science, business, architecture and psychology.

Life at school

4 questions

What is a normal school day like?

Homeroom is at 07:45. Lunch runs 11:20 to 12:55, and it is best to go early because the lines are already packed by 11:20. Some periods are a flipped classroom, where you watch the lesson as a video. On projects with a lot of hardware you sometimes stay at school until about 18:00 to 19:00. Please talk to your parents beforehand, and expect transport to be crammed at that hour.

Where is the AI classroom?

The AI computer room is room 1909 on the 9th floor. Food is not allowed in the room.

How do I get to school?

The school is on Sukhumvit 23, Watthana, Bangkok. Take the BTS to Asok or the MRT to Sukhumvit, bus 38 or 136 to Asok Montri Road, or the Saen Saep canal boat to Asok or Prasarnmit pier. More on Life at EAI.

Is this the official website?

No. It is an unofficial recreation made by a student of the major, not the official website of the school or the major.
